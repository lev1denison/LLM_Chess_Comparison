# My revised study protocol

I study **assisted one-move chess-puzzle accuracy**: whether the model chooses the official solution move when exactly one solver move is required. I supply FEN, a piece list and all legal moves in alphabetical order, without strength rankings. I do not measure unaided move generation, multi-step solving, causal effects or human first-move accuracy.

I freeze 1,000 fresh positions for both GPT-OSS 120B and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite. I exclude all earlier sampled/reserved IDs and solver positions, including the stopped mixed-length bulk study. I allocate 334/333/333 puzzles to the existing low/middle/high rating bands, then freeze 800 training and 200 held-out cases. My first 100 training puzzles are the pilot and remain part of the final study if the protocol does not change.

I record rating, legal moves, piece count, solver-relative material balance, tactical tags, phase, side, FEN, official move and source metadata. Themes are multi-label; my candidate themes and feature-frequency rules are fixed in `analysis_plan.json`. Phase labels use the existing dataset tags, with missing/ambiguous phases labeled unknown. I freeze encoding from training puzzle characteristics before responses. A solution-length predictor is inappropriate because it is constant.

My fuller final regression pools both deployments, includes model identity, and clusters standard errors by puzzle. My smaller early regression includes rating and numeric board characteristics plus identity; the 100-case pilot is too small to promise reliable coefficients for many theme categories. A descriptive early tree accompanies it where supported. My final predictive model is XGBoost, separately fitted per deployment on the fixed 800 training puzzles and evaluated once on 200 held-out puzzles. The new `final_analysis_plan.json` defines 150 depth-3 boosted trees, learning rate 0.05 and fixed regularization. I do not search parameters or use early stopping. I save the model before test prediction and reuse cached evaluations on rerun. The original `analysis_plan.json` stays frozen for the pilot; the new final plan supersedes its final CART and quota sections without changing my primary regression or predictors.

I made this revision after inspecting earlier exploratory results. My local full-solution pilot yielded no complete solutions, and the assisted local first-move pilot yielded few successes. Hosted pilots provided enough successes and failures to study associations. These historical pilots used different samples/prompts and are practical feasibility evidence, not a controlled model ranking or results of this revised study. `background_evidence.csv` contains counts and source hashes.

I completed the same 100-puzzle pilot per model (GPT-OSS 35 correct; Gemini 53 correct). I have not started bulk collection in this amendment. I retain old raw logs under ignored `data/` for evidence, exclusions and quota accounting; they are never pooled into this new analysis. My original PDFs remain unchanged. The new protocol supersedes their outcome, model list and sample size. My final XGBoost model follows the proposed tree-based method, while my early CART figures remain descriptive.

My frozen sample contains 979 mate-in-one-tagged puzzles out of 1,000. That is a limitation of this one-move sample; I do not generalize the results to multi-step puzzles or broad strategic play. Rare tactical themes remain descriptive. I selected theme encoding from training puzzle characteristics before any new responses.

## My bulk-readiness amendment

I recorded this user-authorized final-model amendment after the exploratory pilot and before further collection or held-out evaluation. My 1,000 puzzle IDs, 800/200 split, first 100 answers per model, prompts, generation settings, scoring, board predictors and primary regression are unchanged. I retain the exact early snapshot, fitted models, plots and original source-audit documents.

I removed hard local daily-token/request estimates from the collector. Historical usage remains available for reporting and minute pacing. Real provider headers/errors determine quota pauses; I save each response before scoring, retry short minute limits, and stop that provider at an actual daily limit. Groq retry instructions determine its saved retry time when supplied; absent an instruction, a 24-hour fallback is explicitly labeled an estimate. Gemini daily-quota responses use the documented midnight-Pacific reset. Zero-access or monthly limits require review. My default `WAIT_FOR_QUOTA=False` stops safely for manual continuation later; the other provider can continue independently.

I followed [Groq rate-limit guidance](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits), [Gemini rate-limit guidance](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits) and the [XGBoost parameter reference](https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/parameter.html). I installed and pinned XGBoost 3.4.1. This adjustment makes no new LLM or metadata calls and does not start the bulk run.
